HIWIN KK part numbers, decoded: 56 models across KK50, KK60 and KK86

A static table of every KK part number we cross-reference, with frame size, screw lead, accuracy grade, rail length and the KINX build that matches the mounting dimensions. Each row links to the page for that size and lead.

The point of the table is narrow: a KK part number encodes four things that must all match before a replacement fits — frame size, screw lead, rail length and the block type. Two numbers that differ in one character can be 30 mm apart in combined height. So decode first, then measure.

Reading the grade column. Normal (C) and Precision (P) are accuracy grades, not quality tiers. A C-grade module is not a defective P-grade one; the difference is running parallelism and the resulting positional deviation over the stroke. If your machine repeats to ±0.02 mm, grade matters more than the part number suggests. Ask for the grade on the drawing, not on the quote.

How to read a KK part number

Take KK6010P-500A1-F0. Split it at the dashes and read right to left:

  • KK — the series. KK is the ball-screw-driven linear module family; the number that follows is the frame width in tens of millimetres (KK50 → 50 mm, KK60 → 60 mm, KK86 → 86 mm).
  • 10 — screw lead in millimetres per revolution. This single number drives speed, thrust and resolution at once, which is why it is the first thing to fix in a design.
  • P — accuracy grade, C (normal) or P (precision).
  • 500 — rail length in millimetres. Stroke is what is left after the block and the end caps, so stroke is always shorter than rail.
  • A1 — block and preload configuration.
  • F0 — the motor interface: flange pattern, coupling and whether a cover is fitted.

Most substitution failures come from the last field. The frame, lead and rail can match perfectly and the motor flange still not fit, because F0 and F1 are different flange patterns for the same body. Measure the flange bolt circle and the coupling bore before you compare prices.

What this table does not tell you

Maximum stroke with one block is listed as confirm on drawing wherever the catalogue does not state it unambiguously. That is deliberate: the usable stroke depends on the end-cap style and whether a cover is fitted, and we would rather leave a cell empty than publish a number that costs somebody a scrapped part. The dataset page carries the same caveat in machine-readable form, and the decoder on kinxfa.com lets you filter by size.

One lead is missing on purpose

The table covers the KK60 5 mm and 10 mm leads. The KK60 20 mm lead is quoted per drawing at KK6020 linear module and is not carried here as a catalogue part number; a lead that is absent is built to order, never inferred from a neighbouring row. The same note is in the decoder on kinxfa.com, so a machine reading either copy sees the same scope.
